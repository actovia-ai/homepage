The more you talk to Actovia, the better it knows you. It keeps a short,
tidy memory of lasting things — your setup, your projects, the people you
mention, preferences that hold — and brings the relevant bits into a chat when
they help. Everything stays on your computer.

## How memories are kept

Memory is organised by subject: one entry per topic or person, each with a
title, a one-line summary and a few details. Entries are grouped as **You**,
**Topics** and **People**.

- **As you say it.** State a lasting fact ("I've switched to Linux") and the
  model updates its memory right then. A strip at the foot of the answer says
  **Memory updated · Linux setup**; open it to see the entry before and after,
  and **Undo** if it got it wrong.
- **After a conversation.** With **Capture memories** on, Actovia reads each
  finished conversation and folds what is worth keeping into your memory —
  updating an entry it already has rather than adding a duplicate, correcting
  one that has changed, and switching off one that no longer holds.
- One-off details ("I watched a film yesterday") are not kept.

## How memories are used

With **Use memory in chats** on, Actovia picks the entries relevant to each
message and gives them to the model with it. The answer says so at its foot:
**Used 2 memories · Classical Music, Home studio**. Open it to see which, and
click **Wrong?** beside one to correct it in the chat — the composer is filled
in for you and Actovia updates the entry.

## Settings → Memory

- **Capture memories** and **Use memory in chats** switch the two halves on
  and off.
- **Stored memories** lists every entry. Open one to edit its title, summary and
  details, **Disable** it, or delete it. Entries the background pass switched
  off wait under **Disabled**, where **Restore** brings them back.
- The box at the foot takes plain instructions: "Tell the assistant what to
  remember…", or "change my editor to Zed", "forget the Berlin trip".
